Live Scoring, the Sheet & the Admin Page

A plain-language guide to how court-reported live scores work, why they're never official, and how to use the admin page to clean them up.

Who to call
The ATD is the technical point of contact for this whole system. If anything about the app itself isn't working — a host's PIN won't work, the admin page won't load, a live score looks stuck or wrong — that's a question for the ATD, not something to troubleshoot solo.

The one rule that matters

The Matches Google Sheet is the only source of truth. Nothing on /score or the Live Scoreboard is ever official.

If live scoring goes down entirely — a dead phone, an outage, anything — it has zero effect on the tournament. You keep running off the sheet exactly as before this feature existed.

What court hosts are actually doing

Court hosts log into /score and tap in points as they happen, frame by frame, so spectators not standing at the court can follow along on the Live Scoreboard. When a host finishes a match, they tap “Complete Match” — and the app reminds them, on screen, to fill out that match's Match Report Sheet, get it signed by the winning team, and bring it to you in the DJ Booth.

That's the whole handoff. The app does not notify you automatically. It does not update the sheet. A host finishing a match on their phone changes nothing about your workflow: you still write the final score and winner into the Matches sheet by hand, from the signed Match Report Sheet, exactly as before.

The pending nature of what shows up live

Once a match has no winner in the sheet yet, its court-reported score becomes eligible to appear on the Live Scoreboard, under “On the Courts.” The bracket pages don't show any of this — undetermined slots just stay blank (or, in the Final Four/Championship/Rankings, show “TBD”) until you enter the official result, so they only ever reflect finalized results. “Just Finished · awaiting official score” is the pending state: it's the host's word that the match is over, shown to spectators as informational only, explicitly flagged as unofficial, and still waiting on you. There's no timeout — it sits there until you transcribe the result, however long that takes.

Worth remembering
Always transcribe the final score from that match's signed Match Report Sheet — check it's filled out correctly and signed by the winning team — never from what a court host tapped into the app. The instant you fill in a winner, the match drops off “On the Courts” and into “Past Results” automatically.

The admin page

/admin lets you inspect and clear the in-progress court-score data directly — the same data behind “On the Courts.” Use it to remove a bad or stale entry, or wipe test data, with no CLI access needed. It's not linked anywhere public — reach it by direct URL only.

  1. Go to /admin and enter the Admin PIN — one shared PIN known only to the TD/ATD, separate from individual court-host PINs.
  2. You're in until the login expires (12 hours by default) or you close the tab. It's deliberately not remembered longer than that — this page can wipe every live score at once.

Every entry shows the match ID, court, who's scoring it, when it last updated, and its score — including matches already finalized in the sheet, which stop showing on the public scoreboard but linger here until cleared. Delete removes one row; Clear all wipes every entry at once, with a confirmation prompt first.

Important
Deleting an entry here never changes the official bracket — it only hides a row from the live/in-progress display. There's also no per-person audit trail, since the PIN is shared: don't rely on this page for “who touched what” history. Treat the PIN like a password.

Quick reference

Does finishing on /score update the sheet?No — the host still brings the signed Match Report Sheet to the DJ Booth.
