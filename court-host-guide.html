Keeping Score on /score

How to log in, follow along frame by frame, and wrap up a match — using the exact buttons you'll see on your phone.

The short version

The players keep score on the physical chalkboard — that hasn't changed. This app is a convenience for spectators, not the official record.

Keep it matching the chalkboard as the match goes, and if you notice an obvious mis-call in the moment (“that was an 8, not a 7”), it's fine to say something — that's not considered coaching. When the match ends, fill out the Match Report Sheet, get it signed by the winning team, and bring it to the ATD in the DJ Booth, same as always.

Logging in

There's one link for every host — nobody needs a link specific to their court. Your ATD will send it directly; bookmark it once you have it.

  1. Open the link — you'll see “Court Scorekeeping.”
  2. Enter the name + PIN your ATD gave you.
  3. Tap Log in.

Your login stays active on that device for about 18 hours, so you shouldn't need to log in again mid-tournament. Only people the ATD has set up can log in — there's no free-text name field, so every score update on the board is attributed to a real, verified person. PIN trouble, or the app not working, goes to the ATD — they're the technical point of contact for this whole system.

Picking your match

After logging in you'll see every unfinished match, across all courts. If your login has a home court (or, for most court hosts, a pair of adjacent courts you score together), the list starts filtered to it — tap All / Courts 1–2 / Court 1 / … any time to change it. It's a convenience filter only: you can score any match on any court, say if you're covering for someone.

If another host is actively updating a match, you'll see “Being scored by <name>” under it — a heads-up, not a lock (see “Two hosts, one match” below).

Tapping doesn't save right away. It stages the pick for the frame in progress — you'll see a highlighted button and a “pending” total. Tap as many buttons as you need, on either side, in any order; a frame can have more than one disc land. Undo last pick removes just your latest tap, and Reset Frame clears everything staged so far this frame.

Fell behind a frame or two?
The frames along the way are just there to help you keep pace — they don't each need to be right. Stage enough taps to bring the running score up to whatever the chalkboard actually shows right now, then hit Submit once to catch up in a single step. Matching the current score matters far more than getting every frame in between exactly right.

Tap the big button at the bottom when the frame's done — normally “Submit Frame N & Start Frame N+1.” On a frame that ends the match, it relabels to “…& Complete Match” and finishes the match in the same tap. Nothing landed for either team? Submit still advances — a scoreless “wash” frame is normal.

Disc colors & the frame counter
The header always reads “Playing Frame N of 16” — the score shown reflects everything before frame N, since it's still in progress. Disc colors swap at frame 9: the app updates the dot automatically once you cross that line, so just keep tapping the same team's buttons regardless of which color they're currently shooting.

If the match is tied after frame 16, just keep scoring it the same way — the header switches to “Playing Frame N · Overtime,” and disc colors stay on whatever they swapped to at frame 9 (no swapping back).

Something went more wrong than one bad tap? Reset Match wipes the whole match back to 0–0, Frame 1 — use it only as a real do-over.

Finishing up

Once the match is truly over, tap Complete Match (or it happens automatically on a decisive frame 16+, as above). You'll land on a “Marked complete” screen with the final score.

This is the step that matters most
Fill out that match's Match Report Sheet, make sure it's filled out correctly, get it signed by the winning team, and bring it to the ATD in the DJ Booth. Tapping Complete only updates what spectators see — it does not notify the TD or ATD, write to the official tournament sheet, or advance any bracket.

Tapped Complete by mistake? Reopen (mis-tap) goes back to live scoring.

Two hosts, one match

If you try to save and someone else updated that match in the last few minutes, you'll see:

“<name> is currently scoring this — take over?”

Take over saves your update anyway — use this if you should actually be the one scoring right now. Cancel backs out and loads their latest score instead. This is a real check, not just a warning, so it's worth heeding the “Being scored by” note before you start.
